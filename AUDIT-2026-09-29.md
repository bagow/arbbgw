# Binance/KuCoin execution audit — 2026-09-29

## Scope and conclusion

This audit covers the only enabled route: Binance USD-M perpetuals versus KuCoin
USDT perpetuals. It checks market-data discovery, executable depth, symbol and
contract normalization, sizing, fee/funding budgets, account preflight, order
identity, hedging, exits, accounting, persistent recovery, risk limits, canary
control, and deployment rollback.

The retained production evidence does **not** justify manufacturing more trades
by lowering the profit filter. The first live trade after improving candidate
delivery exposed a separate entry-model defect: a venue-basis direction change
was classified as a large excursion from a near-zero EMA. Trade 2044 was fully
hedged and correctly reconciled, but closed at the ten-minute time limit with a
verified net loss of 0.026781448 USDT. The canary was stopped immediately after
that result; no second trade was allowed under the same rule.

No software can guarantee a profitable fill. A signal can disappear between
the last quote and the matching engine, and a historical snapshot cannot prove
future fills. The release criterion is consequently: more timely *eligible*
candidates, no weakening of the net-entry budget, and verified exchange fills
and fees before a trade is counted as successful.

## Replay evidence

The read-only replay uses 478 snapshots from production build
`1f90f9421b482a67146ec7ee270bd82bd511e0ff`. It models the common lot grid,
complete L2 VWAP, four fees, expected slippage, verified structural reserve,
500 ms non-widening confirmation, exit-side depth, take-profit, hard stop, and
same-pair cooldown.

At a 0.3% structural-deviation reserve, 184 snapshots were eligible and formed
six confirmed durable windows with four independently simulated take-profits and
two unresolved windows. Production-serial selection reduces this to one TP and
one unresolved later cycle because overlapping observations are not separate
trades. The real ledger is consistent with that evidence: one revision-2 trade
closed with verified positive PnL and the following attempt had zero fill.

The subsequent build recorded 791 additional candidate samples and one complete
live trade. Its losing ONEUSDT direction had a 24-hour signed-basis range of
approximately -5.1508% to +4.5306%, `has_structural_spread=false`, and a near-zero
EMA reserve despite a roughly 4% live spread. Waiting longer did not repair the
signal: replay confirmations from 0.5 to 30 seconds still admitted it.

Replaying the zero-crossing regime gate over all 50,001 retained samples removes
all seven independently simulated hard-stop paths while retaining three TP paths
and eight unresolved paths. On the internally consistent pre-change build it
retains the production-serial TP; on the losing build it changes 294 eligible
samples to zero. This is a small, candidate-biased data set, so the result is a
risk justification rather than a profitability guarantee.

Lowering the reserve to 0.1% adds a STARUSDT cycle whose retained path reaches
approximately -0.997% and never demonstrates a profitable exit. Raising it to
0.5% leaves only four eligible samples. The selected 0.3% is therefore the least
bad evidence-backed balance in this small data set; it is not a guarantee and
must be re-evaluated after the new collector accumulates a larger sample.

Replay limitations are explicit: candidate-only snapshots do not prove queue
position, fills, prices between samples, private-account readiness, or listing
age. `UNRESOLVED` is not automatically a loss, and independent overlapping
windows must not be counted as simultaneous real opportunities.

## Release changes

1. Refresh Binance all-symbol best bid/ask once per second instead of once per
   three seconds. This costs 300 documented request-weight units per minute and
   remains below the process hard stop of 2,200 and exchange limit of 2,400.
2. Prewarm one stale Binance candidate book through the official top-5 endpoint,
   globally bounded to two in flight and one start per 500 ms. The resulting
   book does not bypass the 750 ms scanner or final-entry freshness checks.
3. Retry transient candidate private prewarm after five seconds instead of
   quarantining a short-lived opportunity for thirty seconds.
4. Cache stable funding schedules for five minutes, matching the technical
   configuration, while still checking the earliest settlement before entry.
5. Preserve a live canary through transient private-cache gaps; the core rejects
   entries during the gap and the canary fails closed if it lasts one minute.
6. Add a deterministic, read-only opportunity replay and rejection-stage health
   counters so subsequent tuning is based on measured causes rather than waiting.
7. Require a persistent directional structural regime both before L2/private
   prewarm and before final signal creation. Strategy risk revision is advanced
   to 3 so outcomes from the changed entry semantics remain separately auditable.
