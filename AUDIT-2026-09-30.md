# CEX perpetual-futures arbitrage research — 2026-09-30

## Executive conclusion

The running strategy is not locked arbitrage. It opens a long on the cheaper
perpetual and a short on the more expensive perpetual, then waits for their
venue basis to converge. Perpetual futures have no expiry and are not guaranteed
to converge at a known date. This is a leveraged, market-neutral relative-value
strategy with basis, funding, execution, liquidity, liquidation, and exchange
risk.

The absence of new trades is currently correct for the configured profile. A
read-only replay of all 50,001 retained production candidate snapshots found no
eligible entry for the restored production settings (1.2%–2.5% entry, 2% net
take-profit, $6 notional, $3m minimum volume). A sweep of 36 more permissive
profiles did not produce a positive replay result. This retained sample is
candidate-biased and cannot prove future returns, but it gives no evidence that
loosening filters will create profitable volume.

Increasing leverage to 10 changes margin usage and liquidation risk. It does not
create price convergence, reduce four-leg fees, or increase the number of
genuine opportunities.

## What the literature and venue specifications imply

1. Perpetuals have no maturity. Funding is the anchoring mechanism, and trading
   costs create no-arbitrage bounds. A cross-venue premium can therefore remain
   structural instead of converging on the bot's holding horizon.
2. A taker/taker round trip has four fee-bearing executions. At the observed
   Binance 0.05% and KuCoin 0.06% taker rates, fees alone are about 0.22% of one
   leg's notional. The engine's additional 0.08% expected IOC allowance makes
   its minimum estimated cost about 0.30%, before adverse selection.
3. Correct opportunity measurement needs simultaneous executable bid/ask depth,
   not two individually fresh but temporally different books. Queue position and
   fills still cannot be inferred from snapshots.
4. Pre-funded collateral on both CEXes avoids blockchain settlement during a
   trade, but replaces settlement latency with venue counterparty risk and order
   acknowledgement/hedging latency.
5. Microseconds are realistic only for in-process decisions or colocated venue
   access. Public-Internet end-to-end execution from the current VPS is a
   millisecond problem. Nanosecond timestamps in an API measure time precisely;
   they do not promise nanosecond execution.

## Production evidence

The server is healthy as a process but intentionally cannot trade:

- build: `ab2456037bef9e214bd051d1cc434fa0cb7daa56`;
- active profiles: 0;
- persistent kill switch: enabled;
- active trades: 0;
- 13 verified closed trades: -0.417164 USDT net in total.

Trade 2044 demonstrates the execution problem. Its synchronized snapshots did
show a real ONEUSDT venue premium, but entry took 1,522 ms from the first send to
the second observed fill. KuCoin was sent first; Binance was not sent until 514
ms later. The exit was also sequential. The trade remained hedged for about ten
minutes and closed at the time stop for -0.026781448 USDT net. This was not a
phantom L1 signal: it was a persistent basis that failed to converge enough.

Across the retained execution journal, recent KuCoin observations took roughly
0.3–0.6 seconds and Binance observations roughly 0.7–1.0 seconds. One Binance
fill observation took 2.397 seconds. Direct one-off REST requests from the VPS
completed in about 405 ms for Binance and 496 ms for KuCoin. The local engine
cycle can run in hundreds of microseconds, but venue round trips dominate.

## Atomic software findings

### Market data

- KuCoin discovery BBO is real-time, but executable depth is the legacy
  `level2Depth5` feed aggregated at 100 ms for every symbol. The newer Pro feed
  supports candidate depth up to 500 levels at 10 ms.
- Binance candidate depth is 100 ms, but inactive profiles leave only one fresh
  Binance execution book because the active-profile discovery fallback is off.
- `L2Snapshot.updated_ms` is an exchange event time, not a separately recorded
  local receipt time. Both are needed to measure network delay and compare two
  venue states correctly.
- Before this audit, final entry and take-profit valuation allowed two books up
  to 750 ms old independently without bounding their mutual timestamp skew.

### Signal and economics

- A large raw cross-venue spread is often a persistent venue basis, not profit.
  The EMA/regime guard is directionally useful but is not a calibrated forecast
  of convergence probability or half-life.
- Funding timestamps are used to exit before settlement, but the funding-rate
  differential, mark/index premium, volatility, and estimated convergence
  probability are not part of expected-value ranking.
- The strategy optimizes a threshold and later net TP, rather than expected net
  PnL after probability of fill, adverse selection, latency, and time-to-exit.
- With only Binance and KuCoin, genuine executable deviations are naturally
  sparse. Software cannot manufacture many positive-EV trades from two already
  closely linked markets.

### Execution

- The audited production build enters sequentially: it submits one IOC, waits
  until its state is terminal, refreshes the other book, then submits the hedge.
  This creates hundreds of milliseconds to seconds of naked-leg risk.
- The audited production build polls exits once per second and submits them
  sequentially, so a transient profitable close can disappear before the
  second leg is sent.
- Binance already has a persistent WebSocket order lane. The audited production
  build still submits KuCoin orders through REST even though KuCoin's Pro private
  WebSocket supports `futures.order` and matching-system in/out timestamps.
- The audited production entry preflight makes positions, open-orders, fees,
  balances, leverage, and funding calls on both venues after receiving a signal.
  That network batch is incompatible with a short-lived edge.
- A global entry busy flag and the durable unresolved-entry invariant serialize
  entries. `max_active_trades=7` permits several open positions, not seven
  simultaneous order pairs.

### Operations

- Production is still on an older binary than local commit `c5116b0`; its health
  payload still contains an inactive Gate slot even though current source limits
  trading collectors to Binance and KuCoin.
- GitHub Actions failures shown after a few seconds are consistent with account
  Actions quota/billing rejection rather than compilation. Local deterministic
  tests pass, but no new production binary has been installed by this audit.
- A profile with `is_active=0` plus a persistent kill switch explains zero live
  trades regardless of scanner speed.

## Recommended engineering order

1. Preserve correctness first: require synchronized L2 at entry and profitable
   exit, record exchange event time and local receipt time separately, and never
   treat an asynchronous spread as executable.
2. Keep entry as concurrent, independently bounded IOC submissions with a
   durable two-leg state machine and immediate residual neutralization. Do not
   weaken unknown-outcome and partial-fill recovery.
3. Maintain Binance and KuCoin orders,
   balances, and positions from private streams with REST only for startup and
   reconciliation.
4. Use real-time BBO for the full universe and allocate high-rate incremental L2
   only to ranked near-threshold candidates and owned positions.
5. Replace spread-only entry with a calibrated expected-value model using
   executable basis excursion, mark/index premiums, funding differential,
   short-horizon volatility, measured latency, and empirical convergence
   probability/half-life. Train and validate it on event-level synchronized
   snapshots, not candidate-only samples.
6. Use actual account fee tiers and measured per-venue slippage distributions.
   Consider maker/taker execution only after modelling non-fill and asymmetric
   hedge risk; lower fees are not free.
7. Move infrastructure closer to both exchange gateways only after the protocol
   path is WebSocket-based. This can reduce milliseconds, but public APIs cannot
   provide guaranteed end-to-end microseconds.
8. Reactivate only through a small guarded canary after out-of-sample replay is
   positive and a shadow run shows enough synchronized opportunities. Trade
   count is not a release criterion; verified net expectancy is.

## Local corrective patch verified in this audit

- reject entry books whose exchange timestamps differ by more than 250 ms;
- do not trigger profit/hard/trailing exits from desynchronized books;
- reduce supervisor polling from 1 second to 100 ms;
- dispatch both reduce-only exit orders concurrently;
- submit every KuCoin futures order through an authenticated, persistent Pro
  WebSocket with `TCP_NODELAY`, raced IP connection setup and nanosecond exchange
  timestamps; there is no REST order fallback;
- subscribe candidate KuCoin books to the Pro `obu` `increment@10ms` feed and
  accept both text and binary WebSocket frames; entry fails closed unless that
  fast book is fresh;
- keep dedicated pre-warmed Binance and KuCoin order lanes and expose their
  readiness in `/health`;
- remove the 12-call network preflight and Binance REST depth race from the
  signal-to-order path; fees, leverage, funding, and a fresh no-open-orders
  verdict are prepared ahead of the signal, while missing state fails closed;
- persist both order identities and dispatch the two bounded IOC entry legs in
  the same Tokio turn, logging their local start gap in nanoseconds;
- defer ambiguous acknowledgements to the independent reconciliation loop
  rather than blocking the hot path with REST;
- evaluate owned positions every 10 ms while retaining a 100 ms idle cadence;
- apply the same synchronization rule in the offline replay.

Verification: 130 deterministic Rust tests passed (5 explicit live-network tests
ignored), the read-only KuCoin Pro 10 ms depth test also passed against the live
feed, 21 Python tests passed, and the Git whitespace check passed. These changes
remain local and were not used to activate trading.
